# Stage 0 — Interview (when the material has to come from the user)

Run this **instead of** relying on a story bank when the piece is about the user or their own work, or when the user is the only source of the story:
- portfolio posts, About pages, personal LinkedIn posts, founder stories, case studies of their own work, talks, bios;
- any request where the story bank is empty and the user lived the story.

**The user is the story bank.** Your job is to get real moments out of them: scenes, numbers, exact words. Not adjectives.

## The rules

1. **Never offer example answers.** Not "e.g. *I thought design meant conversions*", not a menu of sample stories, not "something like...". A tired user will pick one, and you'll have invented their story for them. Ask open questions and wait.
2. **At most three questions per round.** People give thin answers to long forms. Ask two or three, read the answers, then dig deeper.
3. **Ask for scenes, not summaries.** "Take me to the moment...", "What happened right before that?", "Where were you when you saw the number?"
4. **Follow up on the vague.** "It improved a lot" → "What did it go from, and to? Over how long?" "My manager was impressed" → "What did they actually say?" "I learned to be data-driven" → "What's a decision you made differently because of a number?"
5. **Rough answers are welcome.** Tell the user bullet points, voice-note rambles and half-remembered numbers are fine. You'll do the shaping.
6. **Never fill a gap yourself.** If they can't remember a number, it becomes `[NEEDS SOURCE: what number]`, or the sentence is written without it. Approximate numbers are fine only if the user gives them and the piece says "about".
7. **Respect privacy.** Don't put employers', clients' or colleagues' confidential results in a public piece unless the user confirms they may share them. Ask once: "Are you allowed to share these numbers publicly?"

## Round 1 — the arc (ask these three, nothing else)

1. "What did you believe about this work when you started that you don't believe now?"
2. "Tell me about one specific moment when that changed: what were you working on, and what happened?"
3. "Who is this piece for, and what should they think or do after reading it?"

## Round 2 — dig into the strongest answer

Pick the most promising moment from round 1 and ask 2–3 follow-ups, chosen from:
- "What happened right before that? What did you try first?"
- "What were the numbers, before and after, and over how long?" (and "may these be shared publicly?")
- "What did someone say to you at that point, as close to their words as you remember?"
- "What did you do differently the next day?"
- "What's one detail you still remember: the time, the screen, the room?"

## Round 3 — voice and gaps (optional)

- "Can you paste something you've written that sounds like you: a post, an email, a message?" (This becomes their voice sample.)
- Ask only about the gaps the outline needs. Stop as soon as there's enough for a strong piece: usually one turning point, two concrete details and one exact line.

## Output

1. **Story files** in `.writers-room/story-bank/stories/` (template: `brand-template/story-bank/story-template.md`), `source: interview`, using the user's own words verbatim for quotes. Show them to the user; they become `status: approved` when the user confirms.
2. **A minimal personal brand pack** if none exists: `.writers-room/BRAND.md` with sections 1 (what you do), 2 (who you write for), 4 (voice, plus their sample under section 9) and 5 (anything not to say publicly). Leave the other sections empty; don't invent them.
3. Then continue with `01-brief.md`, using the approved stories as the material for stage 2.

## Gate

- No sentence in the piece describes something the user didn't tell you.
- Every number came from the user, with permission to publish it.
- The user has seen and approved the story files before drafting starts.
