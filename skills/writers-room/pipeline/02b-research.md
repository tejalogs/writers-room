# Stage 2b — Research (facts, sources and perspectives)

Run this when the piece makes claims the story bank can't support: most blogs, landing pages, ads with claims, and every piece in **no-story mode** (explainers, how-tos, comparisons, data posts). Skip it for pieces built purely on the user's own stories.

**Input:** `01-brief.md`, `02-material.md`.
**Output:** `02b-research.md`

## Do

1. **Perspectives first.** List 3–4 people who'd read this piece differently: the target reader, a sceptic, an expert in the field, someone who tried and failed. For each, write the 2–3 questions they'd ask. These questions drive the research and later become H2s and FAQ entries.
2. **Find sources for each question**, if you can browse:
   - Prefer primary sources: the study, the official statistics, the documentation, the company's own data. Not a blog quoting a blog.
   - Note the **publication date**. Anything older than about 3 years in a fast-moving field gets flagged as possibly stale.
   - If two credible sources disagree, record both.
3. **Build the claims table.** Every factual claim the piece might make goes here, and only claims in this table may be stated as fact:

   | # | Claim (as you'd write it) | Source (title, publisher, URL) | Date | Confidence |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | ... | ... | YYYY | high / medium / low |

   *Confidence:* **high** = a primary source says it directly; **medium** = a reputable secondary source; **low** = a single or old source. Low-confidence claims are softened ("one 2021 survey found…") or cut.
4. **Can't browse?** Write the claims you'd want, each as `[NEEDS SOURCE: claim]`, plus the search query that would find it. Don't fill the table from memory: remembered statistics are the most common source of invented numbers. And never fill the gap by inventing a study the brand "ran"; if the brand has its own data, it must be described in the brand pack.
5. **Thin research is a finding, not a failure.** If the table is mostly `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, tell the user at the stage 3 checkpoint, and build the angle on an argument (a contrarian claim, a distinction, a method) instead of on numbers.
6. **Information gain.** List what the top results all say (cover briefly) and what none of them say (cover deeply). In no-story mode, this "nobody says" list is where the piece's surprise comes from.
7. **Unused-material check.** At the end of stage 4, come back and list any strong fact or perspective here that the outline doesn't use. Either use it or cut it on purpose.

## No-story mode hooks

With no scene to open on, the hook comes from the research: the most surprising fact in the table (with its source), a contradiction between two credible sources, or the sceptic's sharpest question, answered directly.

## Gate

- Every number planned for the piece has a row in the claims table, or a `[NEEDS SOURCE]` marker.
- Every row has a date.
- No claim comes from memory alone.
