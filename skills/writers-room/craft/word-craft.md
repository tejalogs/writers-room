# Word Craft — fresh words, no recycled language

Readers stop trusting a piece the moment it sounds like every other piece. Recycled language comes in five forms. Hunt all five.

## 1. Dead phrases (clichés)
Phrases readers have seen so often their eyes slide past: "think outside the box", "low-hanging fruit", "move the needle", "at the end of the day", "the elephant in the room".
**Fix:** say the literal thing in plain words, or find the concrete image only this story has. "The elephant in the room" → "Nobody mentioned the gap on her CV, including her."

## 2. Machine tells (what generated prose sounds like)
- **Stock words:** delve, landscape, navigate, tapestry, robust, seamless, unlock, elevate, leverage, crucial, pivotal, realm, myriad, testament.
- **Stock structures:**
  - "It's not just X, it's Y." / "This isn't about X. It's about Y." (once a piece at most)
  - Triplets everywhere: "clear, concise, and compelling". Use two or four sometimes; use one most often.
  - Rhetorical question then immediate answer, repeatedly: "The result? Growth."
  - Paragraphs that all end on a tidy moral.
  - "Whether you're a student or a CEO..." openers.
  - Heavy em dashes and colons for drama.
  - Every sentence the same length.
- **Fix:** the linter flags the words; the critique flags the structures. Rewrite in plain speech.

## 3. Zombie nouns (nominalisations)
Verbs and adjectives turned into abstract nouns: "make a decision" (decide), "provide assistance" (help), "the implementation of" (implementing), "has the ability to" (can). They drain energy and hide who did what.
**Fix:** find the buried verb and give it a subject. "The optimisation of your CV is required" → "Rewrite your CV."

## 4. Empty intensifiers and hedges
very, really, extremely, incredibly, truly, quite, rather, just, actually, basically, literally, somewhat.
**Fix:** delete; if the sentence weakens, the noun or verb was wrong. "Very tired" → "exhausted". "Really important" → say *why* it matters.

## 5. Abstract nouns doing concrete work
"Challenges", "solutions", "opportunities", "journey", "growth", "success", "value". These words point at nothing.
**Fix:** replace with the specific thing. "Overcome challenges" → "get past the screening call". "Career journey" → "the four years since graduation".

---

## What to do instead

1. **Concrete nouns, strong verbs.** "She *gutted* the CV" beats "She made significant changes to her CV".
2. **Active voice by default.** Passive only when the actor truly doesn't matter.
3. **The reader's words, not the industry's.** Mine them from transcripts. If customers say "no one gets back to me", don't write "low response rates".
4. **Fresh comparisons, or none.** A metaphor must come from the world of the story (the ironing board desk), or be cut. A borrowed metaphor is worse than a plain statement.
5. **Numbers instead of adjectives.** "Huge" → "212".
6. **Short words for hard ideas.** Save complexity for the thought, not the vocabulary.
7. **Read it aloud.** If you wouldn't say it to a friend across a table, rewrite it.

## The swap drill (run in polish)

For every sentence, ask: could this sentence appear unchanged in a different article on a different topic? If yes, it's recycled. Make it belong here, with a detail, a name, a number or the reader's own words, or cut it.
