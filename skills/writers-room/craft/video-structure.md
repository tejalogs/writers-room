# Video Structure — the filmmaker layer

Use this for video ads, short-form videos (Reels, Shorts, TikTok), YouTube intros and explainers, whether filmed, animated or generated by AI video tools.

A writer thinks in sentences. A filmmaker thinks in **shots**: what we see, what we hear, what we read, and for how long. Every line of a video script must answer: **what is on screen right now?**

## 1. The rules of the screen

1. **Show it, then say less.** If the picture carries it, cut the line. Voice-over that describes what we can see is wasted.
2. **The first two seconds decide everything.** The picture, the on-screen text and the first spoken words must all hook at once, and they should agree. Never open on a logo, a title card or a slow establishing shot.
3. **Design for sound off.** Many feed videos are watched muted. The on-screen text and the pictures must tell the story alone. Sound is the bonus layer.
4. **One idea per shot; change shots with the idea.** In short-form, a cut every ~1.5–3 seconds keeps pace (a rule of thumb, not a law). Hold longer only when the moment needs to land.
5. **Motion has meaning.** A push-in intensifies; a pull-out reveals; handheld feels urgent or real; static feels calm or deliberate.
6. **End on the picture they'll remember**, then the call to action. Don't end on a logo alone.

## 2. Shot vocabulary

| Shot | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Wide / establishing** | Where we are. Use sparingly in short-form; it's slow. |
| **Medium** | A person talking or doing; the default for talking heads. |
| **Close-up (CU)** | Emotion: a face, a reaction. |
| **Extreme close-up (ECU) / insert** | The status detail: the declined card, the blinking cursor, the clicking knee. Often the best hook shot. |
| **POV** | The viewer *is* the person: their screen, their hands, their view. |
| **Over-the-shoulder (OTS)** | Conversation, or watching someone see something. |
| **Screen recording** | Software and apps. Zoom in; never show a full desktop at phone size. |
| **Split screen** | Two things at once: before/after, two paths. |

| Move or transition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Push-in | Tension rising, "this matters" |
| Pull-out | Reveal, context, the twist |
| Whip pan | Energy, a jump in time or place |
| Match cut | Connects two ideas by shape or motion (spreadsheet row → train window) |
| Jump cut | Compresses time; fast and modern |
| Hard cut to black / silence | The punchline or the turn |

## 3. Turning storytelling devices into pictures

Pick the device in stage 3 (`pipeline/03-angle.md`), then translate it:

| Device | On screen |
|---|---|
| Parallel paths | Split screen, or intercut A/B with matching shots so the difference is obvious |
| Behind the scenes | POV of the other side: the recruiter's screen, the buyer's checklist (only what's true) |
| Visual metaphor | A physical prop or set: career confusion as a maze, a budget as a leaking bucket. Keep one metaphor per video |
| Confession | Direct-to-camera, medium close-up, minimal cuts: honesty reads as stillness |
| Satire / exaggeration | Escalating repetition, comic timing, a hard cut on the absurd beat. Must read as obvious exaggeration |
| Unexpected revelation | Hold back the key information, then pull out or cut to reveal it |

## 4. Structures

The persuasion arc (`narrative.md` §10), timed:

| Length | Hook | Recognition | Tension / Revelation | Solution | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **15 s** | 0–2 s | 2–5 s | 5–10 s | 10–13 s | 13–15 s |
| **30 s** | 0–3 s | 3–8 s | 8–20 s | 20–26 s | 26–30 s |
| **60 s** | 0–3 s | 3–15 s | 15–40 s | 40–52 s | 52–60 s |

For organic short-form (not ads), end with a **loop** instead of a hard CTA: the last line or image leads back into the first, so a rewatch feels natural.

## 5. The script format (A/V table)

Write video scripts as a table: one row per shot or beat. The linter (`--mode video`) reads this format.

```markdown
| Time | Visual (shot) | Audio (VO / SFX / music) | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | ECU: card reader flashes DECLINED. Petrol pump nozzle still in hand. | SFX: two harsh beeps | Declined. With £2,300 in savings. |
| 0:02–0:05 | CU: her face, then POV: banking app, scrolling subscriptions | VO: I knew exactly where my money went. | 11 subscriptions |
| 0:05–0:08 | Match cut: subscription list → same list, nine rows struck through | VO: I just didn't like looking. | – |
```

Then a **shot list** for the crew or the AI tool: shot number, framing, subject and action, location, props, camera move, duration.

Delivery formats: 9:16 for Reels, Shorts, TikTok and Stories; 4:5 or 1:1 for feeds; 16:9 for YouTube. In 9:16, keep text and faces out of the top ~15% and bottom ~20%, where platform buttons sit (check each platform's current safe zones).

## 6. On-screen text and voice-over

- **On-screen text:** 8 words or fewer per card, on screen long enough to read twice (roughly 1.5 s minimum). It should *add* to the voice-over, not caption it word for word (burned-in captions are a separate, full-transcript layer).
- **Voice-over pace:** about 2–2.5 words per second (130–150 wpm) is comfortable. Above ~2.7 words per second, cut words, not pauses. The linter warns on this.
- **VO for the ear:** every rule in `podcast-structure.md` applies: short sentences, one idea each, numbers said simply.
- **Silence is a tool.** One second of silence before the turn makes the turn land.

## 7. Briefs for AI video generation

For each shot, write the prompt in this order: **subject + action, setting, framing and camera move, lighting and mood, style, duration**.
> "A woman in her 30s holding a petrol pump nozzle, looking down at a card reader showing DECLINED; night-time forecourt, harsh fluorescent light; extreme close-up on the reader, slow push-in; realistic, handheld documentary style; 2 seconds."

- Keep a **character sheet** (age, clothing, hair, setting) and repeat it in every shot so the person stays consistent.
- Never generate a real, identifiable person, a real brand's logo you don't own, or a fake customer testimonial.
- Generate 2–3 takes per shot; choose with the pairwise judge (a large model or a human).

## 8. Truth on screen

- Actors or AI-generated people playing customers must not be presented as real customers. Label dramatisations where your market's advertising rules require it.
- Before/after visuals, health results and financial outcomes: follow the platform's and regulator's rules (see `ads-structure.md`).
- Screen recordings must show the real product, not a mock-up of features that don't exist.
