# Podcast Structure — writing for the ear

Spoken pace is ~150 words per minute. A 15-minute episode is ~2,250 words of script. Listeners can't re-read, skim or see headings, so the script has to do all of that work out loud.

## Formats

| Format | Use when | Script style |
|---|---|---|
| **Solo narrative** (default) | One clear idea, strong stories | Full script, word for word |
| **Two-host** | Debate, myth-busting, warmth | Full script with HOST A / HOST B; the second host voices the listener's doubts |
| **Interview guide** | A guest has the story | Cold open + intro scripted; question ladder; scripted close |

## The default arc (solo, 12–18 minutes)

| Segment | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 0:00–0:45 | Drop into the strongest real moment. End on a question. No intro, no music cue yet. |
| **Ident** | 5–10 s | Show name + one-line show promise. Same words every episode. |
| **Promise** | 0:45–1:30 | What they'll walk away with, concretely. Open the **big loop** (answered in Act 3). |
| **Act 1: the problem as they live it** | ~4 min | The listener's situation, told through a story. Name the belief they hold. Open loop 2. |
| **Act 2: the turn** | ~4 min | The insight that contradicts the belief. Evidence + a second story. Close loop 2, open loop 3. |
| **Act 3: the method** | ~4 min | What to do. Three steps maximum, each with an example, each said twice in different words. Close loop 3, then the big loop. |
| **Close** | ~1.5 min | Callback to the cold open, now seen differently. One action for today. Soft call to action from the brand pack. Tease next episode with a loop. |

Re-hook every ~90 seconds (~225 words): mark `[REHOOK]`.

## Writing for the ear: the rules

1. **Average sentence length 12–16 words; none over 25.** One idea per sentence. The linter checks this.
2. **Say it, then say it again differently.** Repetition is a feature in audio: listeners drift. Key lines appear twice.
3. **Signpost out loud.** "Three things. First... Second... And the third one is the one people miss."
4. **No parentheses, no footnotes, no "as you can see".** If it needs a bracket, it needs its own sentence.
5. **Numbers for the ear:** round them ("about two hundred") unless precision is the point; say them slowly; never more than one number per sentence.
6. **No ambiguous pronouns.** In audio, "she" with two women in the story is confusing. Repeat names.
7. **Contractions always.** "You're", not "you are". People don't talk in full forms.
8. **Read it aloud before it ships.** Any sentence you stumble on gets rewritten. Any sentence that needs a breath in the middle gets split.
9. **Quoting real people:** "She told me: [quote]." Then a beat of silence: `[PAUSE]`.

## Script format

```
[COLD OPEN]
HOST: At eleven forty on a Sunday night, she clicked submit. For the two hundred and twelfth time.
[PAUSE]
HOST: [LOOP: what-she-typed] Then she opened a new tab and typed four words into Google. I'll tell you what they were in a minute.

[IDENT]
HOST: This is <Show Name>. <one-line promise>.

[PROMISE]
...
[REHOOK]
...
[CLOSE: what-she-typed]
```

Delivery markers: `[PAUSE]`, `[EMPHASIS: word]`, `[SLOW]`, `[MUSIC: in/out]`, `[SFX: description]`. Structure markers: `[LOOP: id]`, `[CLOSE: id]`, `[REHOOK]`. All markers stay in the production script; strip them only for a reading transcript.

## Two-host rules

- HOST B is the audience's proxy: asks the question the listener is thinking, pushes back, gets surprised.
- No "Exactly!" / "Totally!" / "That's so true" filler exchanges. Every line advances or complicates.
- Disagreement is gold. Script at least one real pushback per act.

## Interview guide: the question ladder

1. **Scene question:** "Take me to the moment you realised it wasn't working." (Gets story, not opinion.)
2. **Belief question:** "What did you believe back then that you don't now?"
3. **Turn question:** "What changed it?"
4. **Specifics:** "What exactly did you do? What did it say?"
5. **Advice:** "What would you tell someone in that exact spot today?"

Pre-script the cold open (choose it after recording) and the close.

## Episode package (stage 8)

Title (≤ 60 characters, promise + intrigue), show notes (hook + 3 bullet takeaways + chapters with timestamps), 3 clip-worthy moments for shorts with in/out lines, one quote card line.
